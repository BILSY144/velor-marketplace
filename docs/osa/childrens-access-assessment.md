## Stage 1 — Is it possible for children to access the service (or part of it)?

Velor does not currently deploy highly effective age assurance (no age
verification or age estimation technology). Terms state the service is for users
18 and over, but a self-declared age or the mere existence of terms does not
constitute highly effective age assurance under Ofcom's guidance.

**Conclusion: YES — it is possible for children to access the service.** Proceed
to Stage 2.

## Stage 2 — The child user condition

Two limbs, either of which satisfies the condition:

**(a) Is there a significant number of children who are users of the service?**

Evidence available at assessment date: the service is pre-buyer-launch; the only
registered users are approved adult sellers (KYC-verified through Stripe/Payoneer
payout onboarding, which requires adult legal capacity and bank/identity checks)
and a small number of test accounts. Purchasing requires a payment card. There is
no measured child user base because the buyer side has not opened.

Draft conclusion: **No significant number of child users at present.** This limb
must be re-evaluated with real data (e.g. any age-related support contacts,
payment declines, analytics signals) beginning 3 months after buyer launch
(target date for that re-evaluation: 2026-11-06).

**(b) Is the service of a kind likely to attract a significant number of child
users?**

- Nature of content: cultural and artisan goods commerce (ceramics, textiles,
  woodcraft, decor). Not child-directed; no games, no social features live today;
  no content of a kind Ofcom identifies as inherently child-attractive.
- Commercial model requires a payment card to transact.
- Marketing targets adult buyers and adult artisan sellers.
- Comparable services (adult craft marketplaces) are not known for significant
  child user bases.
- Planned Velor Social features (maker journals, collections, follows) are
  commerce-adjacent and maker-focused rather than entertainment content; this
  factor is re-examined in the pre-launch reassessment for each feature, since
  social functionality can change a service's attractiveness to children.

Draft conclusion: **Not of a kind likely to attract a significant number of child
users at present.**

## Outcome (draft — for William's decision)

The child user condition is **not met** at the date of this assessment, so the
children's risk assessment and children's safety duties under Part 3 do not
currently apply. This conclusion is held honestly but cautiously:

1. It is recorded with the evidence above and is reviewable — annually, at buyer
   launch +3 months (2026-11-06), and before each Velor Social feature launch.
2. Velor nonetheless adopts child-protective defaults voluntarily, per the plan
   and the ICO Age Appropriate Design Code posture: if any account is known or
   suspected to belong to a minor it is restricted pending verification; future
   social profiles ship private-by-default; no engagement-maximising nudge
   patterns are used for any user (LAW #4 — healthy by design).
3. If evidence later indicates a significant number of child users, a full
   children's risk assessment will be completed within the statutory window and
   protective measures applied.
