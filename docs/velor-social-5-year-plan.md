# VELOR SOCIAL — The 5-Year Plan

## The Global Social Home of Real Makers

*Prepared 28 July 2026 · Built on five deep research briefs (hook psychology, social commerce winners & losers, cold-start strategy, craft audiences & law, technical architecture) — every claim in this document traces to a real, current source.*

---

## 1. The Vision

Every big social platform sells factory-made goods. TikTok Shop moved $64 billion last year — overwhelmingly mass-produced. Temu and Shein are a race to the bottom. Even Etsy, the one platform that was supposed to belong to makers, has spent twenty years stripping out its community features while raising its take rate to roughly 25% — and its buyers and sellers are now leaving: active buyers fell from 95.5M to 86.5M, sellers from 8.1M to 5.5M, and "handmade" on Etsy is increasingly AI-generated and dropshipped junk.

Velor goes the other way. **We are the global platform for the real makers and artisans the big companies don't entertain** — the carver in Sweden, the weaver in Oaxaca, the potter in Stoke, the brass-worker in Jaipur. Not a store with a feed bolted on. A place where people come to *watch things being made, follow the people who make them, and own a piece of it* — and where every follow, every share, every "how did they make that?" is a step toward a purchase.

The market is real and enormous: the global handicrafts market was $906.8B in 2024, heading for $1.94T by 2033. Craft process content is one of the internet's biggest genres — #handmade has 135.9 billion TikTok views, crochet 15B, pottery 13.5B. Ravelry proved a niche craft community can hold millions of members for 18 years with a 5-person team. And science backs the wedge: buyers pay up to 17% more for goods they know are handmade — the "handmade effect" — because handmade goods are perceived as literally containing love. Story sells. We are the story platform.

**Five-year ambition:** by 2031, Velor is the place the world's artisans post their work first, the place buyers go to "shop the world's cultures," and a self-funding business where every pound of commission finances the next stage of growth.

## 2. Why We Win (What the Research Proved)

**The giants failed at this — twice each.** Amazon Spark: killed. Amazon Inspire: killed 2025. Instagram Shop tab: removed. Facebook/Instagram native checkout: fully dead August 2025. The repeatable lesson from the graveyard: *you cannot bolt a feed onto a store — nobody comes to a store to socialise.* Commerce has to live inside genuinely engaging content, made by people with a real incentive to make it. Our sellers have the strongest incentive there is: it's their livelihood, and process content is their native language.

**The winners tell us exactly which mechanics transact.** TikTok Shop: content IS the storefront; video drives 50% of GMV; 4.7% conversion vs ~2% elsewhere. Whatnot ($8B GMV, +167% in 2025): live drops, scarcity, seller-as-streamer, 80%+ month-over-month retention. Xiaohongshu ($31B valuation): authentic community notes seed trust — 81% of merchant GMV comes from returning customers. Depop: social verbs tied directly to transactions — offers/negotiation are now 40% of all transactions. Poshmark: sellers do the distribution work because sharing boosts their own search rank. Pinterest (619M users, growing fastest with Gen Z): saving and collections — self-built wishlists that retarget themselves.

**The niche wave is with us.** Users are fleeing giant algorithmic feeds for identity-based niche communities: Strava 32M→55M, Letterboxd 6M→26M in two years, Ravelry ~11M registered on a tiny team. Their shared playbook — *come for the tool, stay for the network; retention through identity and accumulated personal investment, not addictive feeds* — is precisely what a small platform can execute, and it happens to be the model regulators are now effectively mandating anyway.

**And the incumbent is wounded.** Etsy's seller exodus over fees and AI-slop is our recruiting pipeline. Our pitch to them writes itself: verified real makers only, genuine community, and a take rate that undercuts 25% by a distance.

## 3. The Hook — How Velor Gets People Coming Back

The research is unambiguous: for a platform our size, the durable hook is **Investment + Identity**, not slot-machine feeds. Variable-reward algorithmic feeds need content volume we don't yet have — and they're now the regulated surface (EU charged Meta over infinite scroll and autoplay in July 2026; the UK's Ofcom requires taming "toxic algorithms" for children). We build the healthy version, brand it proudly, and let it double as compliance.

**The buyer's hook — "my corner of the world's craft":**

- **Collections** — buyers curate boards of pieces ("My cottage kitchen", "Wedding gifts", "Pieces from home"). Useful with zero other users (single-player mode), instantly shareable, and every collection is a self-built wishlist that retargets itself — the Pinterest mechanic, which converts: 85% of Pinterest's weekly users have bought from what they saved.
- **Follows** — follow a maker, and their new pieces and workshop posts reach you. The parasocial bond is the artisan sector's superpower: people don't just buy the pot, they follow the potter.
- **The Workshop Feed** — a scrollable home feed of maker posts: works-in-progress, kiln openings, carving sessions, finished pieces (each linkable to its listing). Chronological first, follows-first; ranked "Top" tab later.
- **Drops** — the appointment mechanic. A weekly "Fresh from the Workshop" drop day concentrates all activity into one moment where Velor genuinely feels alive (Whatnot's core insight: a stream with 30 viewers feels packed; a feed with 30 users feels empty). Live-shopping converts at 9–30% vs 2–3% for standard e-commerce — the artisan-native version is the kiln-opening / workshop drop.
- **Owned collection & gifting** — "pieces I own" registry with care notes and the story of the maker; 44% of handmade transactions are gifts, so gift collections and shareable gift guides are first-class citizens.

**The seller's hook — "the studio journal that sells":**

- **The Maker Journal** — sellers document their work (photos/clips of the process) for their own record; the feed is simply a view over these journals. This is Ravelry's genius: the community content is a by-product of a tool people would use alone. Sellers never "post into the void" — every journal post is guaranteed a first audience (slotted into the weekly digest email, homepage carousel, and category feeds — TikTok's guaranteed-first-200-viewers mechanic, translated to our scale).
- **Followers as owned audience** — unlike Etsy, a Velor seller builds a follower base they keep. That's the hook that makes sellers evangelists: we're not just their store, we're their audience.
- **Status** — Founding Maker badges, "verified real maker" identity, story-first profiles. Status costs nothing and it works (Product Hunt, Quora, Medium all bootstrapped on it).

**Every mechanic above sits in the regulator-approved column** (saves, logs, user-controlled feeds, friend-first content, community, opt-in digests) while the mechanics under legal attack (infinite scroll defaults, autoplay, engagement-optimised feeds for minors, streak punishment) are exactly the ones we skip. "Healthy by design" is both our compliance posture and a marketing line the niche-community wave proves people now actively want.

**Retention targets to hold ourselves to** (social-app benchmarks): D1 40%+, D7 25%+, D30 15%+, with D7 the make-or-break checkpoint; for our hobby-shaped community, weekly ritual (drop day) matters more than daily opens. The number we chase above all, from the best social-commerce retention businesses: **% of GMV from returning buyers** (Xiaohongshu's top merchants: 81%).

## 4. The Five-Year Plan

### Year 1 (now → mid-2027): Foundations & the Atomic Network — "Make it feel alive"

The cold-start research is emphatic: don't launch "a feed" — launch **one atomic network**: the smallest group for whom Velor Social is complete. Slack's atomic network was under 10 people; Ravelry started with fewer than 100 beta users from a knitting blog's readership.

- **Q1: Single-player value + plumbing.** Collections/wishlists for buyers; Maker Journal for sellers; follows; share-out cards (one tap → branded image card to Pinterest/Instagram Stories/WhatsApp with a deep link back — WhatsApp and email drive ~69% of referral shares). Migrate images to object storage first (technical prerequisite — see §7). Complete the UK Online Safety Act paperwork (§8) before any user-generated posts go live.
- **Q2: The Workshop Feed + weekly Drop.** Feed ships scoped (follows + featured makers, so 10–15 active sellers fill it); "Fresh from the Workshop" weekly drop with email digest; in-app notification bell. Founding Makers programme: permanent badge, locked-in lower commission, name on the Founding Makers page — recruit directly from disaffected Etsy sellers and from craft TikTok/YouTube makers who already create process content and want an audience they own.
- **Q3: Community verbs.** Likes, comments (shallow threads), maker Q&A on listings; seller-refers-seller programme with cash rewards (host-refers-host was Airbnb's single most efficient supply lever; cash beats coupons 2.2–2.7×; lead with a concrete earnings estimate).
- **Q4: First live drops.** Scheduled live workshop events (v1 can be a YouTube/Vimeo embed + live chat + drop listings going live at the hour). Measure conversion against the 9–30% live-shopping benchmark.

*Year-1 exit criteria: one craft niche with 25–50 genuinely active makers journaling weekly; drop-day ritual established; D7 ≥ 20%; social-attributed GMV measurable; infra cost of the whole social layer ≤ £10/month.*

### Year 2 (mid-2027 → mid-2028): The Content Engine — "Every maker a channel"

- Native short video in journals (Bunny Stream or Mux — £1–5/month at our scale) — process clips are the genre with 15B+ views on TikTok; ours link straight to the piece.
- Replicate the atomic network into adjacent niches (ceramics → woodwork → textiles → jewellery), one at a time, each seeded with its own founding-maker cohort — Chen's playbook: build one dense network, then copy it, never dilute into one thin global feed.
- Buyer referral programme; collections become followable; gift guides become shareable/SEO pages (programmatic pages from listing metadata: craft × material × region × occasion — dozens to hundreds of *rich* pages, the small-site version of programmatic SEO).
- Mobile app (Expo/React Native — browse, feed, notifications, checkout) once the web ritual is proven; push notifications arrive here, used like scarce currency (personalised, event-driven; more than ~3/week and 40% of users disable them).
- Team addition #1 (revenue-funded): part-time community & moderation.

### Year 3 (2028–29): Live Commerce & the Creator Layer

- Native live drops (auctions/limited releases — the Whatnot mechanics: pre-bidding, scarcity, personality) for the categories where collector culture exists.
- Creator/affiliate layer: anyone can earn commission by curating collections and content that converts — **paid on sales generated, never on posting** (Flip paid for engagement, hit a $1B valuation, and died in 2025; TikTok Shop's 15M affiliates are paid on conversion and thrive).
- Diaspora storefronts: "Shop your heritage" — curated country/culture hubs. Cross-border research: 59% of global shoppers buy cross-border and the #2 motivation is "product unavailable at home" — exactly the diaspora use case; the UK South Asian community alone is a ~£100B consumer economy with festival-driven spikes (Diwali, Eid). Our shipping stack (Shippo + Easyship, 11 origin countries live, enterprise expansion in negotiation) is already built for this.
- Team additions: seller success + content/partnerships.

### Year 4 (2029–30): The Network Compounds

- Maker-to-maker layer: teams/guilds by craft or region (Ravelry's 46,000 groups), skill-sharing, commissions marketplace (buyer posts "I want a custom X" → makers respond — Etsy killed its version of this; sellers still mourn it).
- International seller recruitment at scale through the enterprise shipping lanes; multilingual surfaces.
- Editorial and events: "Meet the Maker" series, craft awards, city pop-ups with local maker communities — the offline bridge that Strava proves supercharges niche-community retention.

### Year 5 (2030–31): The Institution

- Velor as the default global destination for authentic craft: the verification standard for "real maker" (our anti-factory, anti-AI-slop certification becomes a moat and a brand), the trend data source for the sector, the place culture ministries and tourism boards partner with to bring their artisans online.
- Optional by then, funded by then: Velor Live studio formats, creator fund (sales-linked), acquisitions of struggling niche craft communities.

## 5. The Self-Funding Flywheel — "A system that pays for its own growth"

The system is a loop where every stage's revenue funds the next stage's growth, and every growth mechanism is paid on results, never on activity:

1. **Makers post process content** (free for us — it's their livelihood and their journal).
2. **Content converts browsers to buyers** (UGC converts 6.73× better than brand content; handmade story adds up to 17% willingness to pay).
3. **Each sale earns Velor commission** — our only fuel.
4. **Commission funds exactly three growth expenses, all performance-priced:** referral rewards (paid only on a completed referred sale — first referred sales typically arrive ~14 days after launch of a programme), affiliate/creator commissions (paid only on converted sales), and infrastructure (near-zero: the entire social layer runs £0–10/month at current scale, worst case ~£25/month at real traction — see §7).
5. **New buyers follow makers → return** (returning-buyer GMV is the compounding asset; social-commerce leaders run 80%+ repeat rates).
6. **Sellers see followers + sales they can't get elsewhere → recruit other sellers themselves** (seller-refers-seller, cash-rewarded, the proven #1 supply lever) → more content → loop.

Because the marginal cost of the social layer is close to zero and every growth payment is contingent on revenue, **the system cannot outspend itself** — growth spend scales only as sales scale. That is the self-funding property, and it's also the honest answer to "we need a bigger team": each hire unlocks when the flywheel's revenue covers it, with the first hire (community/moderation) triggered by activity volume, not vanity.

What we deliberately do NOT spend on: paid ads (word of mouth is the strategy — 92% trust peer recommendations over ads), engagement bounties (the Flip failure), and fake anything (fake reviews/engagement now carry FTC fines up to ~$51,744 per violation and are banned outright in the UK under the DMCC Act — and beyond legality, the IRL app died of fake users). Seeding is done transparently: founder-posted content and labelled house curation accounts ("Velor Editors"), the ethical version of how Reddit set its early tone.

## 6. Global From Day One

- **Positioning:** not a UK craft site with international shipping — a *global* platform headquartered in the UK. The homepage story is the world's workshops: Japan, Mexico, Ghana, Sweden, India, Peru.
- **Supply:** recruit founding makers per country/craft; the Easyship global lanes (11 origins live: GB, HK, US, CA, SG, FR, NL, DE, AU, NZ, BE — enterprise negotiation open for more) are the physical rails; every new origin country unlocked = a new maker-recruitment campaign.
- **Demand wedges:** (a) diaspora buyers ("shop your heritage" — unavailable-at-home is the #2 global cross-border purchase driver), (b) gift buyers (44% of handmade transactions), (c) the values buyer (sustainability/anti-mass-production, ~10% realised price premium).
- **Trust is the conversion lever for cross-border:** fraud fear (52%), delivery time (46%) and customs (43%) are the top barriers — our maker-verification badge, live tracking (built), and landed-cost clarity attack all three.
- **Compliance posture that scales globally:** UK OSA as home baseline (§8); EU DSA micro-enterprise exemption covers us in Europe at our size (with basic notice-and-action + contact points + moderation-policy terms); FTC endorsement/review rules respected in the US. Design once to the strictest standard, apply everywhere.

## 7. The Technical Build (on our exact stack, at our budget)

Verdicts from the architecture research, sized to Next.js + Prisma + Neon Postgres + Vercel and a <£50/month envelope:

- **Feed:** fan-out-on-read (pull) — one indexed Postgres query with keyset cursor pagination. No Redis, no feed workers. This is the correct architecture below ~100k users; Redis (Upstash free tier) only if p95 ever demands it.
- **Schema:** Follow (composite PK), Post + PostMedia, Like (composite PK, idempotent double-like), Comment (one-level threading), Notification — all additive Prisma models, safely separate from order/checkout tables.
- **Images — the one prerequisite:** current base64-in-Postgres images would make a feed unusable (a 20-post page ≈ 13MB through the DB per request; Vercel's 4.5MB response cap; Neon storage 23× the price of object storage). Migrate to Cloudflare R2 (free tier: 10GB + free egress — £0 for us indefinitely) with upload-time WebP resizing, *before* the feed ships. This also speeds up the existing marketplace.
- **Video:** v1 = YouTube/Vimeo link embeds (£0 — already built for listings); v2 native = Bunny Stream (~£1–2/mo) or Mux (~£5/mo, first 100k delivered minutes free).
- **Notifications:** in-app bell first (a table + a badge, 1–2 days); Resend weekly digest + high-value event emails within the free 3,000/month (100/day) — a daily digest would breach it, weekly is correct anyway; web push waits for the mobile app.
- **Real-time:** none needed — optimistic UI + 60s polling feels live; WebSockets on Vercel are beta and unnecessary for the MVP.
- **Build vs buy:** build. GetStream starts at $499/month for what is, at our scale, days of work on our own stack (open-source references: Munia and the Mastodon schema).
- **Total infra delta:** £0–10/month now; ~£25/month at meaningful traction. The social layer is effectively free — which is what makes the self-funding flywheel mathematically true.
- **Safety rails:** feature-flagged /community routes (can be disabled without touching commerce), pooled Neon connections only, counters in transactions, no foreign keys into the order flow.

## 8. Legal & Safety (done once, done early)

Adding posts/comments/feeds makes Velor a regulated user-to-user service under the UK Online Safety Act — with **no small-service exemption, but proportionate duties**. For a small low-risk service the entire baseline is roughly 2–4 days of documented work, using Ofcom's own free small-service toolkit:

1. Documented illegal-content risk assessment (Ofcom templates, 18 priority offence categories) + children's access assessment — required within 3 months of launching U2U features; reassessed annually.
2. Named compliance owner (William, documented); plain-English terms covering content rules and user protection; report button on every piece of UGC + complaints/appeals route (available to non-users too); fast takedown capability with dated records.
3. Moderation baseline: block/mute, verified-purchase-only reviews, keyword/rate limits on new accounts, IP-complaint route for counterfeit claims, 24–48h action on illegal-content reports.
4. GDPR: DPIA before launch (feed + any children's likely access triggers it); child-safe defaults (private-by-default profiles for minors, no nudge patterns) per the ICO Age Appropriate Design Code; privacy notice updated for follows/profiles/notifications.
5. EU users: DSA micro-enterprise exemption applies at our size — we still designate contact points, run notice-and-action, and state moderation policy in terms.

The real enforcement risk for small services is ignoring Ofcom correspondence and skipping paperwork — not moderation scale. Penalties exist (up to £18M/10% turnover) but Ofcom states plainly it is not out to penalise small services complying in good faith. We do the paperwork before the first post goes live, and "healthy by design" becomes a brand asset rather than a burden.

## 10. What We Measure (honestly, from day one)

- North star: **% of GMV from returning buyers** (benchmark to beat over time: leaders run 80%+).
- Social health: weekly active buyers on drop day; D1/D7/D30 (targets 40/25/15); journal posts per active seller per week; follows per new buyer in week 1.
- Conversion: social-session → purchase rate vs product-page baseline (live drops judged against the 9–30% live-shopping benchmark); share-out cards' inbound clicks (WhatsApp/Pinterest deep links).
- Supply: active makers, maker retention, seller-referred sellers, first-sale time for new makers.
- Integrity: zero tolerance for fake metrics — the IRL lesson is written into how we count.

## 11. Honest Risks

- **Liquidity is the killer risk** — an empty feed is worse than no feed. Mitigation is the whole Year-1 design: atomic network first, feed as a view over journals, guaranteed first audience, weekly drop concentration, transparent house curation.
- **Solo-founder bandwidth** — mitigated by revenue-triggered hiring, Claude leverage, and ruthless phasing (nothing in Year 1 requires daily human moderation at launch volumes).
- **A giant copies us** — they've tried social commerce twice each and failed; our moat is the verified-real-maker community and the trust of a seller base the giants monetise rather than serve. Speed + authenticity is the defence.
- **Regulatory drift** — our healthy-by-design mechanics are on the right side of every current and proposed rule (UK OSA, EU DSA/DFA, US state laws); the annual reassessment keeps it that way.
- **Paying for engagement temptation** — never. Sales-linked incentives only. (Flip: $1B valuation, dead in 2025.)

## 12. The First 90 Days (concrete build order)

1. **Weeks 1–2:** Image migration to R2 (prerequisite; also speeds up the shop). OSA risk assessments + terms + report/complaint tooling (the 2–4 day paperwork, done properly).
2. **Weeks 3–5:** Collections (buyer boards) + Follow (buyer→seller) + share-out cards with OG images and deep links.
3. **Weeks 6–8:** Maker Journal (seller posting: photos, text, video links) + seller profile as a followable channel.
4. **Weeks 9–10:** Workshop Feed (follows-first, chronological, cursor-paginated) + in-app notification bell.
5. **Weeks 11–12:** Weekly Drop #1 — recruit the first 10–15 Founding Makers (Etsy refugees + craft-TikTok makers), seed their journals with real content, send the first "Fresh from the Workshop" digest, and measure everything.
6. **Ongoing from Week 1:** the Founding Makers WhatsApp/Discord circle — the community exists before the features do; its best threads become the feed's opening content.

*The plan in one sentence: build the tool makers would use alone, guarantee them an audience from day one, concentrate the energy into a weekly ritual, pay for growth only out of sales, and scale one dense craft community at a time until Velor is the social home of the world's real makers.*
